Every year it’s the same. The invites start arriving, the family WhatsApp group lights up, and suddenly you’re booking trains, buying a sherwani or a lehenga you’ll wear once, and putting ₹11,001 in an envelope for a cousin you see every five years.

Weddings are some of the best days of the year. They’re also some of the most expensive, and almost nobody plans for them. Wedding season shows up as a surprise every single year, even though it’s on the calendar.

This guide covers both sides of a wedding budget in India: attending other people’s weddings without wrecking your month, and saving for your own without a loan.

## Why weddings break budgets

A wedding isn’t one expense. It’s six or seven, all landing in the same week:

- **Shagun or a gift.** Usually the biggest line, and the one with the most social pressure.
- **Outfits.** Sometimes one, sometimes one for every function.
- **Travel.** Trains, flights, cabs to the venue, and the return trip.
- **Stay.** Often free with family, sometimes a hotel.
- **Small stuff.** Salon, tailoring, a gift for the hosts, food on the way.
- **Leave.** Not a rupee cost if you have paid leave, but it’s a real one. Three days for a cousin’s wedding means three days you can’t use for a trip later.

Each one feels manageable on its own. Together, in the same month as Diwali or a rent hike, they add up fast.

## What one wedding actually costs

Here’s a realistic look at two very different weddings: a colleague’s in your city, and a cousin’s in another state.

| Item | Colleague, same city | Cousin, out of town |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shagun or gift | ₹2,101 | ₹11,001 |
| Outfit | ₹0 (re-wear) | ₹8,000 |
| Travel | ₹500 | ₹9,000 |
| Stay | ₹0 | ₹0 (family hosts) |
| Salon, cabs and small gifts | ₹400 | ₹2,000 |
| **Total** | **₹3,001** | **₹30,001** |

Same word, “wedding”, but one costs ten times the other. That’s why a vague plan like “keep some money aside for weddings” fails. You need to list them.

## Plan the whole season, not one wedding at a time

Most weddings cluster in the cooler months and around auspicious dates, so you usually know by September or October what the season looks like. Sit down once and list every wedding you expect to attend.

When you estimate, **round up.** ₹3,001 becomes ₹3,500. ₹30,001 becomes ₹32,000. Weddings always cost a little more than you think, and rounding up builds a cushion without any extra effort.

Here’s an example season:

| Wedding | Month | Estimate (rounded up) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| College friend, Jaipur | November | ₹17,500 |
| Colleague, same city | December | ₹3,500 |
| Cousin, Lucknow | January | ₹32,000 |
| Neighbour’s daughter | February | ₹1,000 |
| **Total** | | **₹54,000** |

Now the season has a number: ₹54,000. That’s a lot to find in one month. It’s much easier spread out. Start setting money aside in May, and six months of ₹9,000 gets you to ₹54,000 by the end of October, before the first wedding.

If ₹9,000 a month is too much, that’s useful to know now, not in January. Start earlier, trim the plan, or both.

## Ways to trim without being rude

You don’t have to skip weddings to spend less on them. A few things that work:

- **Re-wear and rotate.** Nobody remembers what you wore at a wedding in a different city last year. Two or three good outfits can cover a whole season.
- **Book travel early.** Wedding dates are fixed months ahead. Train and flight prices around peak wedding dates usually only go up.
- **Match the shagun to the relationship, not the venue.** A lavish wedding doesn’t mean you owe a lavish gift. Give what fits how close you are and what your budget allows.
- **Go in on a gift together.** For a colleague or a college friend, a group gift from five or six people often feels bigger than six separate envelopes.
- **Pick which functions you attend.** You don’t have to go to the haldi, the mehendi, the sangeet and the reception. Each extra function can mean another outfit and another cab.

Wedding season often overlaps with festival season, too. If Diwali and two weddings land in the same few weeks, plan them together. Our [Diwali budget guide](/blog/budget-for-diwali) walks through the festival side.

## Log it as you go

Weddings are where tracking falls apart. You’re busy, you’re with family, and you’re paying for ten small things a day. By the time you’re home, you’ve no idea where ₹6,000 went.

Log each spend when it happens. If you fall behind, catch up in one go that evening. In [Aviary](/), you can type “shagun 2101, cab 450, salon 800” and [Ask Aviary](/features/ask-aviary) logs all three at once. Whatever tool you use, a quick log on the day beats guessing a week later. ## Saving for your own wedding

Your own wedding is a different scale of problem. It’s a lakh-plus expense with a date you can usually see a year or two out. That makes it a perfect fit for a sinking fund: pick the total, divide by the months you have, and save that every month. Our guide to [sinking funds](/blog/sinking-funds-india) explains the method in detail.

### Step 1: decide who pays for what

In India, weddings are usually a family project. Parents on both sides often contribute, and expectations vary a lot between families. Have this conversation early, before anyone books a venue. It’s awkward for an evening, and it saves months of tension.

Say the total plan is ₹10,00,000, split like this:

| Source | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Parents | ₹5,00,000 |
| You | ₹3,00,000 |
| Your partner | ₹2,00,000 |
| **Total** | **₹10,00,000** |

### Step 2: turn your share into a monthly number

Your share is ₹3,00,000. If the wedding is 24 months away, that’s ₹12,500 a month. If it’s 18 months away, it’s about ₹16,700. The earlier you start, the smaller the monthly number.

Make it an envelope called Wedding and fund it on salary day, like rent. Each month, move the money into a separate savings account, an FD or an RD, so it builds up and stays out of reach. This isn’t money to put somewhere risky. You need all of it on a fixed date.

### Step 3: give the total a rough split

Here’s a starting split for a ₹10,00,000 wedding. It’s not an industry benchmark. It’s a sensible first draft that your own families will reshape.

| Category | Share | On ₹10,00,000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venue and catering | 40% | ₹4,00,000 |
| Outfits and jewellery | 20% | ₹2,00,000 |
| Decor and flowers | 10% | ₹1,00,000 |
| Photography and video | 8% | ₹80,000 |
| Guest travel and stay | 8% | ₹80,000 |
| Music, mehendi and makeup | 5% | ₹50,000 |
| Invitations and return gifts | 4% | ₹40,000 |
| Buffer | 5% | ₹50,000 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **₹10,00,000** |

Venue and catering is usually the biggest line, and it scales with the guest list. Cutting 100 guests often saves more than any other decision you’ll make. The buffer matters too. Something will cost more than quoted. Better that it comes from a planned buffer than a credit card.

### Step 4: don’t let the wedding eat your emergency fund

It’s tempting to pour every spare rupee into the wedding. Don’t empty your emergency fund to do it. You’ll start married life with no cushion, right when you might be setting up a new home. Keep the two separate. The [emergency fund calculator](/tools/emergency-fund-calculator) tells you what your floor should be.

And try to avoid a personal loan for the wedding. A few days of celebration followed by years of EMIs is a hard trade. If the numbers don’t work, shrink the plan before you borrow.

## Getting started this week

Whether you’re a guest this season or planning your own, start here:

1. **List every wedding you expect** in the next six months, with a rounded-up estimate for each.
2. **Add them up** and divide by the months you have left. That’s your monthly Weddings envelope.
3. **Fund it on salary day,** before eating out and shopping get a say. The [salary budget planner](/tools/salary-budget-planner) helps you find room.
4. **If your own wedding is coming,** have the who-pays-what talk with family, then set up a separate Wedding fund.
5. **Log every wedding spend on the day,** so you know what’s left for the next one.
